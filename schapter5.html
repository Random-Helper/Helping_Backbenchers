What is Force?

Force is a push or a pull that can change the motion, speed, direction, or shape of an object.

Force is measured in newton(N)

It can do the following:

  • Move an object at rest, like opening a door.
  • Change the speed of a moving object, for example pushing a swing.
  • Change the direction of motion, like kicking a football coming towards you.
  • It can change the shape of an object, for example playing with clay.
  1. If the same amount of force is applied to an object from opposite sides the net force is 0, meaning the object will not move.
  2. If unequal amount of force is applied, the net force is the difference between the 2 forces, and the object moves in the direction of the larger force.
  3. Lastly if forces are applied on an object in the same direction, they will add up and act on the object.
Examples:
  • In arm wrestling two students apply the same force so their hands don't move
  • If two students push each other the weaker one gets pushed back
  • Two students carry a table and both push it forward, so their forces combine and the table moves forward.

Magnitude: The sum of all the forces acting on an object, from all the directions.

Types of Forces

Contact forces

These forces only act when they are directly in touch with the object. Examples of them are Muscular Force and Frictional Force.

Muscular Force

The force we exert using the muscles of our body, which includes: Running, Playing, Lifting your bag, etc.

Frictional Force

The force that generates when two surfaces rub against each other, it is caused by the roughness of the surfaces because at a microscopic level nothing is totally flat. When these irregularities interlock it opposes motion generating friction.

The amount of friction generated depends on roughness, area of contact and weight. The more the roughness, area of contact or weight the greater the frictional force.

Fluid Friction(Drag)

A fluid is something that flows, so the friction applied by fluid mediums such as air and water is known as fluid friction/drag.

Examples
  • Fish swimming encounter fluid friction because of the water.
  • Meteors start burning due to the fluid friction the air particles generate.
Aerodynamics

Objects moving through fluids experience risistance, but this resistance can be reduced by a streamlined body(sharp at the front and broad at the centre).

Advantages of Friction(The ones in our book)

  • It is necessary for an object to move.
  • It helps bring a moving object to a stop by applying brakes
  • The friction between a matchstick and a rough surface ignites the matchstick.

Disadvantages of Friction

Due to the heat generated by friction tyres and machines get damaged and have to be replaced

Reducing Friction

Friction can be reduced by using a lubricant, which is a substance that fills the irregularities and reduces roughness. For example grease and oil act as lubricants.

Non-Contact Forces

Forces that to not require direct touch or physical contact to push or pull an object such as: Magnetic Force and Electrostatic Force

Magnetic Force

The force exerted by a magnet on certain materials is known as magnetic force, for example a magnet pulling in metal.

Electrostatic Force

The force that is exerted by charged objects is called electrostatic force, a object gets charged when it gains or loses electrons. Charges can be of two types negative and positive. Like charges repel each other while unlike charges attract each other.

The triboelectric series

An arrangement of different materials according to how easily they gain or lose electrons.

Uses of static electricity

It is used in printing technology, painting technology, and in air purifiers. It is used to attract particles either to a specific place or away from in each of these cases.

Gravitational Force

The force exerted by any object that has mass, the more mass a object has the higher gravitational force it exerts. For example the force exerted by the Earth and The Sun.

Centrifugal Force

An outward force that pushes an object away from the centre when it moves in a circular force. In the case of the Earth, the Sun's gravitational force provides the inward pull that keeps Earth in its orbit by balancing the centrifugal path.

Mass and Weight
  1. Mass is the amount of matter an object contains.
  2. Mass can be measured using a Beam balance and kilogram(kg) is the SI unit for measuring it.
  3. Weight is the gravitational force experienced by an object.
  4. The SI unit for measuring weight is newton(N), we can measure it using a Spring balance.
  5. Weight is 9.8x of the mass of an object.

Buoyant force and Gravitational force.

Object experience an upward force called buoyant force when in a liquid. If the buoyant force is greater than the gravitational force the objects floats and if gravitational force is greater the object sinks.
